The Marlow footbridge closure: what happened and what it means

In March 2026, Marlow District Council closed the Kingfisher footbridge after an inspection found corrosion in two of its four main cables. The bridge, which carries about 3,000 pedestrians a day between the railway station on the east bank and the market square on the west bank, has been shut ever since.

What the inspection found

The engineering report by Halden Associates, published on 12 March 2026, rated the bridge "unsafe for public use in its current condition" and recommended that all four cables be replaced. It found that corrosion had reduced the cross-section of the two worst cables by roughly a third, and it estimated the replacement at £1.8 million.

The report also traced the corrosion to a drainage fault: water had been pooling at the west anchorage since at least 2019, when a previous inspection first noted the problem.

How the council responded

The council closed the bridge on 14 March, two days after the report was published. At its meeting on 2 April, councillors voted 9 to 3 to fund the full replacement rather than a temporary repair, with work to start in the autumn. The minutes record the leader of the council saying that "a temporary fix would be throwing good money after bad".

Since the closure, foot traffic has moved from the east bank to the west bank, and the market square traders say takings are down by a fifth. The Marlow Gazette reported that the council has promised a shuttle bus by 1 May.

Why this matters beyond Marlow

The Kingfisher bridge is one of 40 cable-stayed footbridges of the same design built across England in the 1990s. Halden's report says the drainage detail that failed at Marlow is common to the whole series. Nobody has yet inspected the others.

Bridge closures like this one are becoming more frequent, and the maintenance backlog on small structures is the fastest-growing problem in local transport budgets. Councils should inspect every bridge of this design before the end of the year.
