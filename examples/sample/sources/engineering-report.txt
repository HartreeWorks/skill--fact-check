Halden Associates
Structural inspection report: Kingfisher Footbridge, Marlow
Client: Marlow District Council
Report date: 10 March 2026

1. Summary

The Kingfisher Footbridge is a cable-stayed pedestrian bridge opened in 1996, spanning the River Marlow between the railway station (east bank) and the market square (west bank). Following the routine inspection of 24 February 2026, Halden Associates carried out a detailed examination of the four main stay cables and their anchorages.

We rate the structure unsafe for public use in its current condition. Corrosion has reduced the effective cross-section of cables C2 and C3 by between 30 and 35 percent. Cables C1 and C4 show surface corrosion only.

2. Findings

2.1 Cable condition. Cables C2 and C3, which are anchored at the west abutment, show pitting and section loss consistent with prolonged exposure to standing water. Cables C1 and C4, anchored at the east abutment, are within tolerance.

2.2 Cause. Water has been pooling at the west anchorage because the drainage channel installed in 1996 discharges uphill of the anchorage box. A note in the 2019 principal inspection records "standing water observed at west anchorage; drainage to be reviewed". No remedial work is recorded.

2.3 Wider relevance. The same drainage detail was used on the 40 footbridges of this design built between 1994 and 1999 under the county bridge programme. We recommend that the owners of those structures inspect the anchorages.

3. Recommendations

3.1 Close the bridge to the public until the cables are replaced.
3.2 Replace cables C2 and C3 as a minimum. We recommend replacing all four cables so that the structure has a uniform remaining life.
3.3 Reconstruct the west anchorage drainage.
3.4 Budget estimate for full replacement of all four cables and drainage works: £1.8 million, excluding VAT and traffic management.

4. Usage

Council pedestrian counts for 2025 give an average of 2,900 crossings per day, rising to 4,100 on market days.
